# Editorial guide: how hooks, captions and hashtags are written

Claude reads this file every time it writes the text for a clip. Edit it freely: change
the rules, add examples, add notes at the bottom. Nothing else needs changing.
To keep your own version safe from skill updates, copy this file to
`<your clips folder>/editorial.md`; that copy is used instead when it exists.
After editing, run the same `clip` command again with `--rewrite`: only the text is
redone, the picked moments stay.

## Voice

- Write like a person who watched the clip and is telling a friend what happens in it.
- Plain, specific, calm. Say what actually happens; let the clip do the shouting.
- Lower-key beats louder. If a line would also fit a thousand other clips, rewrite it.
- Match the speaker and the context. A dry joke gets a dry line. A real scare can be blunt.
- Use the speaker's name only when it is known from the title or channel. Never guess one.
- Write in the language of the transcript.

## Hook (the title bar on the video)

- 4 to 10 words. One line of thought, no full stop at the end.
- State the setup and hold back the payoff: "He jokes about logging out, then the server
  crashes" is fine because the crash is the setup for the reaction, not the punchline.
- Sentence case. Capital letters only where normal writing has them (names, "I", acronyms
  like GTA).
- No emoji.
- You may mark ONE short phrase with stars to be shown in the accent colour, for example
  `He bets his whole car *on one coin flip*`. Mark the part that carries the tension.
  Leave the stars out when nothing stands out.
- Must be true to the clip. No promise the clip does not keep.

## Caption (the text under the post)

- One or two short sentences, under 140 characters in total.
- Add something the hook did not: a detail, the context, or a dry observation.
- Do not ask the viewer a question, and do not tell them to do anything.
- No emoji unless the settings allow them.

## Hashtags

- 2 to 4. Specific first: the game, the server or series, the creator's name.
- At most one broad tag (like #gaming), and only if it fits.
- Never guess a game or a name that is not clear from the title or the transcript.

## Reaction lines (comments style, when there is no real chat)

- 2 to 6 words, lower case, like a thought said out loud while watching.
- React to the exact second, not to the clip in general.
- They are shown as on-screen reactions, never as viewer comments: no usernames, no
  "first!", nothing that reads as if someone posted it.
- No emoji unless the settings allow them. No all-caps lines; one capitalised word at
  most, and only when the moment is genuinely loud.

## Examples

Good hooks:
- He jokes about logging out, then *the server crashes*
- She parks the car *in the ocean*
- The cop pulls over the wrong car twice

Weak hooks (and why):
- "INSANE moment you won't believe" (says nothing about the clip)
- "He did WHAT??" (shouting, no information)
- "The funniest GTA RP moment ever" (a claim, not a description)

Good captions:
- The timing could not have been worse. He had just finished the sentence.
- Three people in the car and nobody mentions the cliff.

Weak captions (and why):
- "Has this ever happened to you? Comment below" (bait)
- "The timing is actually criminal" followed by three emoji (filler)
